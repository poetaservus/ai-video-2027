# YouTube Advertising & Operating Costs
**Research note for AI Video Monetization Plan 2027 | Verification: 16 Nov 2026**

## Scope and evidence
“Advertising cost” has two different meanings here: (1) money spent by Roland/Attila to buy YouTube ads promoting the channel, and (2) revenue the channel may earn from ads shown to viewers after YPP approval. They are not interchangeable. Official Google/YouTube help pages were directly retrieved on the date above. They define billing, revenue shares and budget controls, but publish no fixed universal CPV, CPM or creator RPM price. Any local rate forecast is therefore uncertain; use account-specific tests and Studio reports, not a supposed guaranteed industry rate. All 2027 costs below are planning examples, not quotes.

## 1. Creator-side advertising revenue
YouTube states there are no guarantees of how much, or whether, a partner will be paid. Under the Watch Page Monetization Module, YouTube pays creators 55% of net ad revenue from public watch-page videos. Shorts have a separate allocation; YouTube states creators receive 45% of the amount allocated to them from the Shorts Creator Pool. These rates are contract terms, not RPMs. RPM is creator revenue per 1,000 total video views after revenue share and other adjustments; CPM is advertiser cost per 1,000 ad impressions/playbacks as defined by the relevant report. Not every view receives an ad, and geography, season, format, ad availability, invalid-traffic adjustments, claims and suitability can move realized RPM. AdSense settlement and applicable tax withholding further separate Studio estimates from cash received. Before full watch-page YPP approval, model YouTube ad revenue as €0; eligibility thresholds are not approval or guaranteed earnings. Do not infer an RPM from CPM or apply the 55% share to gross campaign spend.

## 2. Cost to buy YouTube ads (optional promotion)
Google Ads does not publish one fixed YouTube ad rate: placement, bidding strategy, audience, country, competition, format and creative affect auction prices. With cost-per-view bidding, a billable TrueView view is format-specific; for skippable in-stream ads it is generally 30 seconds (or the full ad if shorter) or a qualifying interaction/click. In-feed and Shorts view definitions differ. The public view count may include paid-ad views; Google explicitly says that public ad views are not the billing metric. Treat paid views as a separate acquisition cohort and judge incremental watch time, returning viewers and conversions—not headline view-count lift.

Budget control: Google Ads uses an average daily budget. For most campaigns, a day may spend up to twice that amount to follow traffic, while billed cost is capped at 30.4 × average daily budget per month. Thus a €5 average daily budget implies a billed-cost ceiling of €152 per 30.4-day month (not an expected bill or guaranteed delivery); two such campaigns can approach €304/month. A €10/day cap implies €304/month. Set start/end dates, billing alerts, account access limits and total campaign budgets where available; review actual billing, because daily delivery can fluctuate. These examples assume a standard campaign and no tax, FX or billing adjustments; verify account controls before launch.

**Recommendation for this venture:** paid YouTube media is an optional experiment, not a required launch cost. Start at €0/month. Do not buy views to make the channel look popular, and do not count campaign results as proof of organic demand. Promote only a proven video to a defined audience with a landing/conversion goal and tracking. If both founders approve a test, cap it at €150 total (illustrative one-off test budget, not a market-price benchmark), document objective and stop date, and compare against the channel’s own organic baseline. Assess incremental qualified viewers, watch time from eligible organic traffic, returning viewers, subscriber quality, site conversion and cost per outcome. Paid views do not replace YPP’s valid public watch-hour requirements; ad-campaign watch time is excluded from the long-form threshold. Stop if the test only increases low-retention views or obscures the organic read.

## 3. Operating overhead: current plan baseline
Use the existing Pilot Production Budget.md as the source-of-truth planning model, not this note as a second budget. Balanced cash baseline per month/video: subscriptions/storage/software €190 fixed + bookkeeping/admin €100 placeholder + production €320 per episode = €610 for one episode/month; paid promotion is **€0** by default. Separately: setup €1,050 once; legal/accounting advice reserve €500–€1,000 (unquoted); 15% first-pilot contingency €249; founder labor ~100 hours × €25 = €2,500 imputed. The €100 admin line is not a verified fee; obtain an accountant quote. At one episode/month, each optional €150 campaign would raise cash outlay to €760 for that month. At the existing assumed €5 RPM, covering €760 from monetized views alone would require 152,000 views (760 ÷ 5 × 1,000), versus 122,000 for €610; this is arithmetic sensitivity after YPP approval, not a forecast, and campaign views themselves are not an investment return. Keep paid promotion outside the recurring overhead and episode production estimates unless a test is explicitly authorized. No CPM/CPV benchmark is inserted because none could be substantiated with accessible authoritative pricing; record actual spend, billable results and conversions from the Google Ads account if tested.

**Current spending guardrails:** no paid promotion by default; one balanced video/month; monthly production+fixed cash ceiling €700 from the main plan (current estimate €610); no commitment beyond the €3,000 initial cap including advice/contingency. If a promotion test is approved, count the full ad spend inside the monthly €700 ceiling and pause other optional spend. Reconcile billed cost monthly; tax, VAT, exchange and founder payments are outside this media-cost illustration.
