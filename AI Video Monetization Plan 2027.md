## 4. YouTube monetization (official snapshot)
YouTube Help, read 16 Nov 2026: expanded YPP (where available): 500 subscribers, 3 valid public uploads/90 days, and either 3,000 valid public long-form watch hours/12 months or 3m valid public Shorts views/90 days; it grants eligible fan-funding/Shopping features, not watch-page ad revenue. Full ad-sharing threshold: 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 valid public long-form watch hours/12 months or 10m valid public Shorts views/90 days. The official YPP overview retrieved 16 Nov 2026 also states updated YPP terms take effect 1 Feb 2027 and must be accepted in YouTube Studio by 31 Jan 2027 to continue fully monetizing; recheck the notice in Studio before launch because terms and dates may change (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851?hl=en). The official YPP page also says updated terms start 1 Feb 2027 and creators must accept in YouTube Studio by 31 Jan 2027 to continue full monetization; check the Studio notice and actual terms before launch. Shorts-feed hours do not count toward 4,000; private/unlisted/deleted/ad-campaign views are excluded. Also require YPP-available country, no active Community Guidelines strikes, 2-step verification, advanced features, policy compliance and linked active AdSense for YouTube. Channel review is whole-channel; the current official page says decisions are typically returned in about one month, but delays and additional review are possible. Thresholds do not guarantee acceptance. If rejected, the page states creators may appeal within 21 days or reapply after 30 days; for later rejections/reapplications, the stated waiting period is 90 days. Before appealing or reapplying, address the cited channel-wide policy issues and retain evidence of original research, authorship and rights. Keep creator uploads active; 6 months inactivity may risk monetization removal.

AdSense: create/link only through YouTube Studio, follow identity, address, tax and payment verification prompts; legal payee and tax reporting need to match Roland’s actual arrangement, not a presumed joint venture. YouTube may show ads outside YPP; that does not mean creator receives a share. Watch-page ads, Shorts revenue, Premium, fan funding, sponsors and affiliate sales have distinct eligibility/terms. Ad revenue is a later-stage possibility, not launch cash.

AI content: YouTube requires disclosure for realistic synthetic/altered depictions of real people/events/places (including realistic scenes that never happened and AI music as focal content). Non-realistic animation, production assistance, idea/script/thumbnail aid, captions, and cloned own-voice narration are listed as examples not requiring disclosure; list not exhaustive. Disclose when uncertain. AI narration is neither automatic approval nor rejection. YouTube monetization policy requires original/authentic work, not repetitive, generic, mass-produced or interchangeable template content. Reused-content review is separate from copyright permission: licensed material can still fail monetization if edits add little original value. Make research/scripting/visual authorship legible: show original charts and diagrams, cite sources in descriptions, preserve working files, add creator commentary and vary each episode’s substance. Permission to upload ≠ lawful rights ≠ YPP eligibility ≠ approval ≠ earnings.

Compliance checklist: obtain licenses for every image, font, music, SFX and footage, confirm commercial YouTube use/territories/term, keep receipts/attribution and source logs; use only claims-cleared script. Declare paid promotions in Studio and clearly disclose sponsorship/affiliate relationships in video and description under applicable law/platform rules; use platform paid-promotion disclosure. Ad-suitability review applies to video, title, thumbnail, description and tags; educational context matters but does not assure full ads. Avoid gratuitous violence, graphic tragedy, sensationalism, hateful content and strong profanity in title/thumbnail. Sources: YPP eligibility and monetization policies; AI disclosure; advertiser-friendly guidelines (URLs in sources section).

## 6. Technology, pipeline and cost method
Recommendation: start with DaVinci Resolve Free (edit/audio/color), Blender (charts, 2D/3D when useful), Inkscape (vectors), Krita, Audacity, LibreOffice, shared cloud storage, and a single paid TTS only after blind voice test. Canva free for drafts; avoid redundant CapCut/Canva/Adobe bundles. Premium Resolve Studio, After Effects or paid stock only if a tested time/quality gain justifies it. Pricing/license terms vary by region and change; not live verified in this research. Confirm commercial use and voice rights before subscribing. TTS shortlist: ElevenLabs (Starter page lists commercial licensing; compare voice quality, pronunciation and plan rights before selection), Azure AI Speech and Google Cloud TTS (API, usage billed; voice quality varies), local Piper (low recurring cost, voice/model license varies). Primary: test ElevenLabs paid tier only if naturalness and pronunciation beat a human-recorded or budget baseline; fallback: local Piper or free Resolve narration by a founder. Make 60-second blind tests, maintain pronunciation dictionary and voice setting log; never clone a third party without documented consent. Pricing snapshot verified 16 Nov 2026: ElevenLabs lists Starter at US$6/month with a commercial license and 30k credits/month; Creator at US$22/month and 121k credits/month (USD before taxes; credit/minute equivalencies and plan limits apply). Google Cloud TTS lists Standard/WaveNet at US$4 per 1M characters, Neural2 at US$16/1M and Studio at US$160/1M; an 8,000-character 10-minute script is roughly $0.03 / $0.13 / $1.28 respectively, before API/account costs. Voice quality and commercial rights must be verified for the exact model/plan. These figures are a dated snapshot, not a 2027 quote. Free local Piper has no inference fee but check the exact voice/model license. Resolve’s official product page describes its integrated edit/Fusion/Fairlight workflow; it did not expose a localized Studio price in the page extract. Model TTS at €0–€30/month until a blind test is complete.

Stock/music: first source original graphics; if necessary use provider subscription on one-month terms, read exact license/Content ID restrictions. YouTube Audio Library is a low-cost first choice, but check each track’s attribution/usage terms. Artlist/Epidemic Sound licensing is plan- and channel-specific; save license proof. Pipeline: one monthly video, 8–12 min. Topic scoring (audience question, source availability, narrative tension, visual potential, rights risk) → research dossier (6+ credible primary/authoritative sources) → 900–1,500-word script, claim table with citations → independent fact-check → scene storyboard → narration with pronunciation pass → custom vector charts/maps/timelines + sparse licensed footage → motion graphics/edit → sound mix → frame-by-frame factual/legal QC → title/thumbnail variants → publish → 48h/7d/28d analytics review. Automate transcription, rough captions and repetitive exports only; human approves every claim, voice, image and final cut. Typical labor target 70–105 hours/video incl. research and QA; learning curve may exceed it.

Folder/version convention: /YYYY/episode-slug/{01_brief,02_sources,03_script,04_audio,05_assets,06_project,07_exports,08_licenses}. Keep immutable release copy, dated source archive, licenses/invoices, asset creator/source/rights expiry, voice/text prompts and project version metadata. Back up source and final render in two locations. QA gates: fact claims traceable; narration natural; no rights gaps; subtitles checked; no unlicensed marks/assets; no unsupported synthetic realism; 1080p H.264/AAC, 24/30fps consistent, peaks controlled, watch full export.

## 11. Risk register and investment gates
High: slow growth/zero revenue; copyright/license claims; YPP refusal for repetitive/reused content; cross-border tax errors; founder dispute. Medium-high: weak narration/retention, research error, platform dependence, burnout/cash strain. Mitigate: original scripts/art, rights ledger, claim/source review, alternate human voice test, budget cap, signed agreement, monthly records, diversify distribution only after channel workflow stabilizes.

Initial cash cap €3,000 for one balanced pilot, 15% contingency and up to €1,000 professional-advice reserve; fee estimate is unverified and tax payments are excluded. No debt. Fixed/admin monthly cap €350; all-in monthly cash cap €700 for one balanced video until pilot review. If quotes or required work exceed these caps, pause and seek both founders’ approval rather than skipping compliance. Stop if unapproved spend, unresolved rights or legal structure, >120 founder-hours/video twice, or cash cap exceeded. Pilot pass: publish only after rights/tax confirmation and no factual red flags; within 30 days post-publish review comments, retention and audience signals; invest further only if founders still consent, labor is trending below 100h, no material rights issues, and either audience demand shows repeatability across ≥3 uploads or an actionable packaging/story fix emerges. Stop after six published videos or 12 months if no improving retention/returning-viewer trend, no plausible path to YPP, and no sponsor/affiliate evidence. Pivot topic/format only after identifying a specific audience mismatch. Revisit cap at quarterly review.
